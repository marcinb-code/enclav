Make the most of your GPU infrastructure.

The right deployment is not determined by model size alone.

We evaluate model requirements, available VRAM, quantization, context length, concurrency and inference software before selecting a hardware configuration.

Depending on the model and workload, deployment may use a single GPU with approximately 80–96 GB of VRAM or a multi-GPU configuration.

Example hardware platforms

NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell

96 GB GDDR7 — Blackwell platform with NVFP4 support.

NVIDIA H100

80 GB — use compatible inference formats such as FP8, BF16 or W4A16 where supported by the selected model and software.

NVIDIA A100

80 GB — compatible BF16 or W4A16 deployment paths.

Different GPU architectures support different numerical formats and inference paths.

NVFP4 is considered only for compatible hardware and software configurations. For H100 and A100 deployments, alternative supported formats such as W4A16, FP8 or BF16 may be appropriate, depending on the model and serving stack.

We do not promise that every model will fit on every 80–96 GB GPU.

When planning, we also account for memory needed for context, KV cache, runtime overhead and concurrent requests.

Scale inference around your workload.

Running AI in your own infrastructure changes how inference capacity is managed.

Instead of relying on a public API provider's RPM quota and per-token pricing, you control the available resources and serving configuration.

This can be particularly valuable for high-volume, repetitive workloads.

We focus on:

  • Throughput per GPU.
  • Concurrent requests.
  • Queue management.
  • Batch processing.
  • Token generation efficiency.
  • Cost per completed task.
  • Infrastructure utilization.

Self-hosting does not mean unlimited capacity.

Performance is constrained by GPU resources, model size, serving software, context length and workload complexity.

The economic advantage must be evaluated against hardware acquisition or rental, power, maintenance and engineering operations.
