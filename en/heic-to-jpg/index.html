    FAQ and limitations

    Why does Windows require an additional extension to open HEIC photos?

    On some Windows PCs, viewing HEIC photos requires additional components: HEIF Image Extensions and an HEVC decoder. The HEIF extension is free, while the standard HEVC Video Extensions app in the Microsoft Store is paid. Some computers already have the required codec installed by their manufacturer.

    You don't need to install additional Windows codecs to convert photos with HEIC➜JPG. Our converter decodes HEIC and HEIF images directly in your browser, and the resulting JPG files can be opened in Windows without HEIC support.

    Does the conversion really work offline?

    Yes. After the page has loaded, you can disconnect from the internet. Multiple-file conversion and ZIP export continue to work because the conversion engine runs locally in your browser using WebAssembly and a Web Worker.

    Is EXIF metadata preserved?

    Full EXIF metadata is not preserved in JPG files.

    How does the converter handle Live Photos?

    The converter accepts the HEIC/HEIF photo, but not the MOV video paired with a Live Photo. The JPG contains the image from the HEIC/HEIF file; the video is not part of the result.

    What about very large files?

    Large photos may require significant device memory during conversion. Practical limits depend on your device and browser. The quality and long-edge settings control the dimensions and quality of the output JPG.

    Want to support the project?

    The converter is free and runs entirely in your browser. If you find it useful, you can support its development by buying us a coffee. It's completely optional.
